Research

Asymptotic frameworks for large stochastic and strategic systems: mean-field control and games, analysis on Wasserstein space, and interacting particle systems, with machine-learning methods that connect theory to computation.

Research themes

  • 01

    Hamilton–Jacobi equations on Wasserstein space

    Comparison principles for viscosity solutions of Hamilton–Jacobi equations on Wasserstein spaces.

  • 02

    Interacting particle systems on sparse graphs

    Singular local interactions through hitting times, motivated by systemic risk in financial networks.

  • 03

    Numerical methods for mean-field control and games

    Machine-learning algorithms for mean-field optimal control and multi-agent games, with convergence guarantees.

  • 04

    Mean-field control in generative AI

    A mean-field control view of wide neural networks and the implicit regularization of training.

Journal articles

  1. Learning Algorithm for Mean Field Optimal Control

    H. Mete Soner, Josef Teichmann, and Qinxin Yan

    SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis, 2026.

    To appear Code Theme 03
    Summary

    We study numerical methods for solving mean-field optimal control problems by leveraging machine learning techniques. A convergence proof based on propagation of chaos and Rademacher complexity is provided. We also discuss a universal approximation theorem for neural networks on Wasserstein spaces.

  2. Iterative Schemes for Markov Perfect Equilibria

    Felix Höfer, Mathieu Laurière, H. Mete Soner, and Qinxin Yan

    Mathematical Finance, 2026.

    To appear Code Theme 03
    Summary

    We study Markov perfect equilibria in continuous-time dynamic games with finitely many symmetric players. We prove the convergence of both Picard and weighted Picard iterations, yielding efficient computational methods. Numerical experiments confirm the effectiveness of algorithms based on this approach.

  3. Viscosity Solutions of the Eikonal Equation on the Wasserstein Space

    Summary

Preprints

  1. Soft-constrained Schrödinger bridge for generative AI: numerical algorithms and approximation guarantees

    Jin Ma, Ying Tan, Renyuan Xu, and Qinxin Yan

    Preprint, 2026.

    arXiv:2611.20312 Theme 04
    Summary

    We replace the terminal constraint in the Schrödinger bridge problem with a penalty for missing the target distribution. We characterize the optimal control through a McKean–Vlasov forward-backward system, derive error bounds for empirical targets and finite-particle approximations, and test two neural methods for generative modeling.

  2. Finite-player Optimal Stopping Games: Randomization, α-potentiality, and Learning

    Xin Guo, Mehdi Talbi, and Qinxin Yan

    Preprint, 2026.

    arXiv:2608.18355
    Summary

    We use randomized stopping rules to study equilibria in finite-player games. Under weak interaction, we construct an approximate potential whose error decreases inversely with the number of players. An exact-potential example gives an explicit equilibrium for testing the learning algorithm.

  3. Optimal Loss Allocation and Reflected Free Boundaries in Systemic Risk

    Yucheng Guo and Qinxin Yan

    Preprint, 2026.

    arXiv:2608.18113
    Summary

    When a bank defaults, how should a planner distribute its losses among the surviving banks? We formulate this as a singular mean-field control problem and study how the optimal allocation shapes the distribution of banks’ distances to default.

  5. Policy Gradient for Continuous-Time Mean-Field Control

    Erhan Bayraktar, Martin Hernandez, Qinxin Yan, and Yuhua Zhu

    Preprint, 2026.

    arXiv:2605.20718 Theme 03
    Summary

    We propose a policy gradient method for entropy-regularized mean-field control in the discounted infinite-horizon setting. We consider randomized feedback policies and a coupled representative-particle/population system, in which the representative state evolves jointly with a population law governed by a McKean–Vlasov equation.

  6. Implicit Regularization of Large Neural Networks via Mean-Field Formulation

    Beatrice Acciaio, Jakob Heiss, Gudmund Pammer, and Qinxin Yan

    Preprint, 2026.

    arXiv:2603.20892 Theme 04
    Summary

    We provide a theoretical formulation to explain the generalization property of overparameterized neural networks through implicit regularization of the training process. We consider the mean-field formulation of wide neural networks, and propose an equivalent mean-field control formulation for the loss function and the corresponding gradient flows.

  7. Particle Systems with Local Interactions via Hitting Times and Cascades on Graphs

    Yucheng Guo and Qinxin Yan

    Preprint, 2025.

    arXiv:2505.18448 Theme 02
    Summary

    We investigate particle systems with singular local interactions through hitting times on sparse graphs, motivated by problems from systemic risk in financial networks.
